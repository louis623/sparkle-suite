# My Website: redesign concepts summary

All three concepts use Fizz Festival demo content and the real theme art from `public/amethyst/skins/*` (hero posters), with palettes taken from `lib/amethyst/skin-cards.ts` / `PRESETS`. Each one renames the page to one plain name, **My Website**, shows the site address with **View my site** and **Copy link**, puts the theme at the top with a try-on preview that doesn't publish, labels every field with where it shows on the site, and replaces the top-only Save with a **sticky save bar** (unsaved count, what changed, Discard, Save & publish). Visual language matches the Dance Floor concept B / Nic-Nac rebuilds: amethyst header card, magenta kicker, Playfair headings, DM Sans body, 22px cards, pink gradient buttons.

Screens: `concept-*-390.png` (Fold cover / phone), `-880.png` (Fold inner), `-1440.png` (desktop). Concept C also has `concept-C-tryon-390.png` and `concept-C-tryon-1440.png` showing the try-on dialog open.

## Concept A: Theme studio + site-flow sections (`concept-A-theme-studio.html`)
The page opens with a **Choose your theme** studio: filter chips (Holiday, Fall, Everyday, Colors, Halloween), theme cards that each show a mini homepage with the rep's own headline, and a large preview of the selected theme (phone frame on phone and the Fold inner screen, browser frame on desktop) with **Use Rose Champagne** / **Keep Silver Bells**. A slim "2 things left to finish" bar sits above it. Below that, everything else becomes numbered collapsible sections in the same order customers scroll: 1 Top bar, 2 Hero, 3 Upcoming shows (links to Calendar), 4 What is a Bomb Party, 5 About you, 6 Sign-up & discount, 7 Footer & socials. Then come Other pages (Dance Floor / Join / Live Lineup switches). Desktop adds a sticky site-map rail.
- **Pros:** Closest to what Louis asked for. Theme is clearly the star, the rest follows the site, and it's familiar and easy to scan. It works the same on every screen size. It's the lowest build risk, since it's mostly regrouping existing controls into `<details>` sections plus a new theme gallery and preview.
- **Cons:** You only see edits to text in the preview if you scroll back up to it, because the preview lives in the theme studio. It's still a long page on phone, but sections are collapsed.

## Concept B: Split editor with live preview (`concept-B-live-preview-split.html`)
An editor on the left and the rep's real site on the right, updating as they type. Desktop gets a browser-frame preview with Home / Dance Floor / Join tabs and a Phone/Computer switch. The Fold inner screen gets a phone-frame preview. On phone, a docked mini preview sits at the top with a **Full preview** button. The theme is a compact rail at the top of the editor, and "Jump to" chips move between parts of the site. The part being edited is outlined in the preview ("Editing · Hero"), and tapping a part of the preview opens its editor. The other sections are a tidy settings list.
- **Pros:** The most "professional tool" feel (like Shopify's or Squarespace's editors). It shows every edit (theme, headline, ticker, photo) before saving, which answers "see exactly what it's going to look like" in the strongest way.
- **Cons:** It needs a true live draft preview, which is the hardest piece technically. On a 390px phone the docked preview takes about a third of the screen. The theme gets less room than in A or C.

## Concept C: Guided setup + checklist (`concept-C-guided-checklist.html`)
The page is a progress-driven setup: "7 of 9 steps done" with a progress bar and chips for what's left. On desktop a sticky step rail shows checkmarks. Step 1 **Pick your look** shows big theme cards split into **In season now** (Christmas / Thanksgiving) and **Year-round**, each with **Try it on**. That opens a bottom sheet on phone or a dialog on desktop, showing the rep's site in that theme in phone and desktop frames, with **Use this theme**. Finished steps collapse to one-line summaries with Edit. Unfinished steps open with friendly guidance (for example, an "Add a photo of you" drop zone that explains why it matters).
- **Pros:** Best for new reps and for nudging reps to finish their sites (photo, store link). The "in season now" grouping fits how reps actually switch themes. Try-on is a clear, separate moment.
- **Cons:** It feels more like onboarding than a daily control panel once a site is finished. The step framing works less well for quick edits like changing the ticker. The try-on dialog hides the rest of the page.

## Recommendation
**Build Concept A as the base, and borrow two pieces:** C's **Try it on** full-screen dialog (for the phone, where the inline preview is smaller) and C's **in season now** grouping in the theme filter. Keep B's tap-to-edit live preview as a later phase. A gives Louis everything he asked for (theme at the top, a real preview before saving, site-ordered controls, a polished tool look) with the least build risk, and it matches the Dance Floor / Nic-Nac card language reps already know. The completion bar from C is already in A as the slim "things left to finish" strip.

Quick wins to ship with A regardless: sticky save bar with Discard and a leave-page warning, "Shows on" labels, View my site / Copy link, link out to Calendar and Team settings, field-level errors (store link) with focus, removing or fixing the dead **Hero motion** select, and dropping internal theme codes from labels.

## How the theme preview could be built (technical)

**Thumbnails (cheap, no server):** render cards from static data. Use the theme's existing hero poster (`public/amethyst/skins/<theme>/hero-poster.webp`, 13+ themes) or a CSS palette art block for color-only themes (Morganite, Moonstone, Emerald Garden, Rose Gold, Garnet, Amber, Velvet, Black Diamond, Alpine Opal), plus the rep's draft headline in the theme's heading font. Add a small preview data map next to `skin-cards.ts` (poster path, bg/fg/accent, button colors). Don't load 19 iframes. Risk: **low**.

**Full "try it on" preview (real SSR page, nothing saved):**
- Add a **server-only, signed-in route**, for example `GET /nic-nac/site-preview?theme=<id>&page=homepage|trade|join`, that loads the signed-in rep's own settings, overrides only `appearancePreset` in memory, and renders through the same path as the public page (`app/[publicSiteSlug]/route.ts` → `renderAmethystPublicAssetResponse` → `preview-template-data.ts`). It never writes. The Workspace shows it in an `<iframe>`.
- Reuse the hardening already in `app/skin-preview/[skin]/[page]/route.ts`: `Cache-Control: no-store`, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`, `frame-ancestors 'self'`, `connect-src 'none'`, `form-action 'none'`, and sandboxed srcdoc. Check the theme against `isAmethystSkinSelectionAvailableToRep` so private themes stay private.
- Phase 2 (Concept B, live text too): `POST` the current draft (or put it in a short-lived signed token) so headline, ticker, about and photo edits appear before saving. Debounce re-renders around 400ms.
- **Don't** add a `?previewTheme=` param to the public URL: anyone could use it, and CDN or page caching could serve the wrong theme to customers.

**Risks to handle:** (1) Preview page views must not count as **site visits** (there's a Site Visits panel), so skip tracking in preview. (2) Sign-up and other forms, and any background data refreshes, must be off in preview (the skin-preview CSP already blocks network calls). (3) Hero video loops and music themes (Silver Bells, Starlight, Pine) should start paused in preview. (4) Reps on custom layouts (Britt with Bling, Bling Kitchen, Mile High Fizz) will preview their custom layout, not the standard one, and the Mile High Fizz layout is triggered by text fields, so the preview should reflect that honestly. (5) Each tap is one server render, which is fine with debounce and no caching. Overall risk: **low–medium** for the theme-only preview (new read-only route reusing existing render code). **Medium** for the live-draft preview.
