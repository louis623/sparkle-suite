# Bri's Sparkle Suite Workspace Welcome Guide — Rebuild Brief

**Purpose:** Rebuild Bri's rep-facing Sparkle Suite welcome guide for her
onboarding conversation. This is a guide for **Bri joining and using Sparkle
Suite**. It is not a guide for people joining Bri's team, and it must not
include Team Management or New Rep Onboarding material.

**Model:** Brittany's thorough twelve-chapter Workspace welcome guide, adapted
to Bri's actual starting point and current product language.

## Grounded starting point

- Bri's customer-facing business is **Bri's Glowtique**.
- Her public site is already served on `brisglowtique.com`; this guide should
  introduce how to shape and maintain that site, not present domain connection
  as a task for Bri to complete.
- Her Workspace is private. Her public customer site and private Workspace must
  be explained as two connected, different places.
- Existing notes describe a grandfathered billing relationship. Do not publish
  an amount, payment link, or billing instruction until Louis confirms the
  current terms privately.
- Do not include passwords, a meeting link, a Live Queue code, email address,
  shipping address, account ID, or payment URL.

## Guide job and tone

The guide should feel like a calm companion for tonight's conversation: warm,
practical, and plain-English. It should show Bri a stronger, more polished
setup without making her feel she has homework to finish before she can use it.
Every product claim should connect to a better customer experience, smoother
live-show flow, or less behind-the-scenes patchwork.

Avoid generic software language, feature hype, roadmap promises, or content
about recruiting/training a downline.

## Proposed page structure

### 1. Welcome, Bri

Open with a short personal welcome and the simple promise: Sparkle Suite helps
her give customers a more polished place to find her, follow her shows, and
stay connected. Reassure her that tonight is a guided tour, not a test.

### 2. Your two Sparkle Suite spaces

Explain the distinction clearly:

- **Bri's Glowtique** is the public customer site at `brisglowtique.com`.
- **Sparkle Suite Workspace** is Bri's private place for managing the parts of
  that customer experience.

Use a simple two-card visual. Do not call the Workspace a customer website or
suggest shoppers sign in to it.

### 3. What is already in place

State only the confirmed foundation: her public site and custom domain are
already connected. Frame the rest as things she can shape at her own pace:
her About section, photos, social links, show information, and the tools she
wants to use.

### 4. Start here tonight

Give a short, optional agenda:

1. Open the Workspace and meet Nic-Nac.
2. Look at Bri's public site together.
3. Add or refine the first details she wants customers to see.
4. Review her next live in Calendar.
5. Walk through Live Queue only when she is ready to use it.

Make clear that checking off guide items is local to the device and does not
update her Workspace or mark a required task complete.

### 5. A quick map of the Workspace

Introduce the current navigation in plain language:

- **Nic-Nac:** practical in-product help for setup, show flow, and everyday
  questions.
- **Dance Floor:** the trade space, where Bri remains in control of every
  decision.
- **Calendar:** where upcoming shows can be organized for customers to find.
- **Tools:** the place for supporting Workspace tools, including the customer
  list where available.

Mention the in-app Message Center as a Workspace communication surface only if
it is available in Bri's account at the walkthrough. Customer email and text
updates must remain visibly labeled **Coming soon** unless Louis confirms they
are ready for Bri.

### 6. Make Bri's customer site feel like hers

This chapter should be the practical heart of the guide. Explain that Bri can
edit her customer-facing details in Site Settings and see the impact on her
public site. Cover:

- business basics, links, social handles, and show details;
- the About section: an optional title plus a paste-ready narrative;
- the **1,200-character** maximum for the narrative, so it remains balanced
  next to her photo on larger screens;
- portraits and optional short-form video/social moments only when Bri wants
  to use them;
- the site preview and autosave behavior.

Do not tell Bri that all fields must be complete. Empty optional content should
be treated as a future opportunity, not an error.

### 7. Put upcoming lives on the Calendar

Explain how Calendar helps customers know when and where to find her. Keep the
walkthrough centered on a real upcoming show or a practice entry. Customer
watch actions use the social links Bri has intentionally saved; if a link is
not configured, the guide should say it can be added later rather than promise
a watch button.

### 8. Live Queue, when Bri is ready

Describe Live Queue as a live-show support tool, not an ordering or payment
system. It can help make queue visibility easier during a live, while Bomb
Party orders and payments stay outside Sparkle Suite.

The public guide may link to the verified official Chrome Web Store listing
only after it is rechecked. The pairing/sync code belongs in private guided
setup, never on the guide.

### 9. Dance Floor stays in Bri's control

Explain the customer benefit without overselling: customers can request an
eligible trade, and Bri chooses whether to approve or decline it. This keeps
the decision with her while giving the customer side a clearer place to engage.

### 10. Everyday support: Nic-Nac, Help, and messages

Position Nic-Nac as Bri's practical Sparkle Suite helper—not a generic
chatbot. Include starter prompts:

- “Give me a tour of my Workspace.”
- “Help me update my About section.”
- “Help me add my next live.”
- “Walk me through Live Queue setup.”
- “Help me set up my first Dance Floor items.”

Point Bri to Help & Resources for step-by-step guidance. If Message Center is
available, describe it as the way Sparkle Suite can communicate inside the
Workspace; do not imply customer email or SMS sending is enabled.

### 11. What can wait

Use individual **Coming soon** cards for any unavailable customer email/SMS
updates and any other feature that is not confirmed ready for Bri. No dates,
no launch promises, and no vague “we are building everything” language.

Add a small “You do not have to do this tonight” note for optional photos,
social links, About copy, and Live Queue setup.

### 12. A confident first week

Close with an optional, device-local checklist:

1. Review the customer site from a shopper's point of view.
2. Add the About title and narrative when ready.
3. Confirm the social links customers should use.
4. Add the next live to Calendar.
5. Ask Nic-Nac for help with one task.
6. Set up Live Queue during a calm practice session, not in the middle of a
   live show.

End with a short reassurance: Bri does not have to master the entire Suite at
once; each small improvement makes the customer experience feel more like her.

## Keep out of this guide

- Brittany's team-only **New Rep Onboarding** flow, private invite links,
  participant progress, supply binder, or “Ask Brittany” conversation.
- Recruiting, downline training, or Join My Team guidance.
- Credentials, codes, billing links, addresses, or unconfirmed prices.
- A domain-migration checklist; Bri's custom domain is already connected.
- Promises that Sparkle Suite places orders, takes payments, or decides trades.

## Before publishing or using it as a walkthrough handout

1. Confirm whether Bri already has an existing public welcome-guide URL and
   update that guide rather than creating a duplicate.
2. Confirm which Message Center/Tools sections are enabled for Bri at the
   meeting.
3. Recheck the official Live Queue Chrome Web Store listing if it will be
   linked.
4. Confirm any meeting, billing, or fulfillment details privately; omit them
   from the public guide unless separately approved.
5. Validate the finished guide on desktop and phone, including navigation and
   every public link, before publishing.
